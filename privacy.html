Privacy

This page explains which personal data is processed when you visit starnotch.app, when you join the waitlist and when you use the Starnotch app, for what purpose, on which legal basis and through which services.

Julio Koelle

2. This website

The website is a set of static pages hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc., 88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. When you open a page, GitHub processes technically necessary data: your IP address, the time, the page requested, and details about your browser and device. GitHub may process this data in the USA.

Purpose: delivering the website and keeping it secure. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. The legitimate interest lies in operating the website safely and reliably.

To show when the app was last updated, the home page asks GitHub's public API (api.github.com) for the date of the app's latest release or change. Your browser sends this request to GitHub directly, so GitHub sees your IP address as it does for any page. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, the legitimate interest being to show current information about the app.

The website sets no cookies, loads no external fonts and embeds no third-party content. If you use the switch between the light and the dark look, your browser keeps that choice in its local storage so the page remembers it. If you open the site through someone's waitlist link (starnotch.app/?ref=…), your browser keeps that invitation code in its session storage while the tab is open, so the invitation still counts after a reload; it is removed once you have joined. Nothing else is stored in your browser.

To know how many people visit and which buttons they use, the pages load Umami Cloud (Umami Software, Inc.), a cookie-free visitor count. It records the page, the referring site, the browser type, the device type, the country derived from the IP address, clicks on "Install", "GitHub", "Copy", "Join the waitlist" and the buttons for copying and sharing your waitlist link, and the moment someone has joined the waitlist, with where on the page and whether through an invitation link. It never receives the name or email address you enter, nor your invitation code. The IP address itself is not stored: Umami combines it with the browser details into a code that changes every day, so a visitor can't be recognised across days or identified. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. The legitimate interest lies in understanding, in aggregate, how the website is used.

4. The waitlist

If you join the waitlist for the full Starnotch app, we store your first name, your email address, the time you joined, where on the page you signed up (the top of the page or the waitlist section) and the version of the consent text shown under the form. Each entry also gets a personal invitation code for your own link (starnotch.app/?ref=…), and if you joined through someone else's link, we note whose link it was. Nothing else: no IP address and no browser details are stored with your entry. Giving your first name and email address is voluntary; without them you can't join the waitlist.

Your place in line: from these entries we work out each person's place on the list. Whoever has brought more people through their link comes first; otherwise, whoever joined earlier. After you join, the page shows you your place and your link. If an address is entered that is already on the list, the page shows that entry's place and link, but not the name. The person whose link you used only sees their own place move up, never your name or address.

Before you join, to show how many people are already on the list, the page asks the same database for that number when you scroll near the form. That request contains nothing about you; like any request, it reaches Supabase's gateway with your IP address (see "Service" below). Legal basis for this request: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, the legitimate interest being to show how many people have joined.

Purpose: telling you about the launch of the full Starnotch app, letting people in early in the order of their place on the list, and addressing you by your first name. The invitation code and the note on who invited whom serve only to count invitations for that order. We don't use your data for anything else and don't pass it on to anyone beyond the service named below.

Legal basis: your consent, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, which you give by sending the form.

Confirmation: before we send you the first launch email, we email you once to ask you to confirm your address (double opt-in). You only get launch emails if you confirm; addresses that aren't confirmed are deleted.

Service: your browser sends your entry directly to our database at Supabase (Supabase, Inc.), which stores it on our behalf as a processor (Art. 28 GDPR). The database runs on servers in Frankfurt, Germany (EU). Like any web server, Supabase's API gateway, which runs through Cloudflare, briefly logs technical data about each request, such as the IP address and the browser, to operate and secure the service. Supabase, Inc. is based outside the EU.

Storage period: we keep your entry until we stop sending launch updates or until you withdraw your consent, whichever comes first, and then delete it. When an entry is deleted, the note on the entries that came through its link is removed with it.

Withdrawal and deletion: you can withdraw your consent at any time with effect for the future, through the unsubscribe link in any of our emails or by writing to julio@juliokoelle.com. We then delete your entry. Withdrawing doesn't affect the lawfulness of the processing before it.

5. The Starnotch app

Starnotch has no account and no telemetry. The developer receives no data from the app. Most features work entirely on your Mac; the ones below send data to a third party, and only when you use them.

On your Mac only. The app reads Claude Code's and Codex's local session files (under ~/.claude and ~/.codex) read-only to show your sessions. Calendar events are read on your Mac after you allow calendar access. Music control talks to Spotify or Music through macOS automation. Permission prompts from Claude Code and Codex reach the app over a local socket only processes of your own user can use. The app never reads the Keychain or browser cookies.

Tara's memory. Tara keeps what you allow (meetings and calendar, quick captures, your notes folder, clipboard, agent sessions) in the app's folder on your Mac. In Settings › Tara's memory you can pause her memory, switch single sources off, keep apps out and delete what she remembers per source. The Dashboard and the memory map show this data on your Mac only.

Ask Tara. When you ask Tara a question, the question and what she needs to answer it (relevant meeting memory, quick captures, connector snapshots, and, if you drop her on a window, a picture of that window) go to the AI you chose: Claude through Claude Code on your own plan, ChatGPT through OpenAI's Codex app on your own plan, or Anthropic's API with your own key; that provider processes them under its terms and privacy policy. With Apple Intelligence or "simple rules" nothing leaves your Mac. The same choice applies to the short summaries Tara writes in the background (work blocks, profiles of the people in your notes), which you can route to a different one of these. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing the feature you requested).

Your meeting tools. Tara learns from meetings through the tool you already use, and you choose which ones: a meeting-notes service you connect (today Jamie, Granola, Wispr Flow and Fathom; this list grows as more are added) or Starnotch's own recording. A connected service is read with your permission, on a schedule and after your meetings; its own privacy policy applies to your account there. Starnotch reads only what you connect and keeps it in Tara's memory on your Mac. You can disconnect any tool at any time.

Starnotch's own recording (optional). Only while you run it: audio goes to a transcription service (today Reson8); the transcript and your meeting memory go to the AI you chose for the live analysis. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR. Make sure everyone in the meeting agrees to being recorded.

Synced lyrics. If you switch them on, the app asks LRCLIB (lrclib.net) with the track's title, artist, album and length.

Tara's memory in your AI apps. If you choose AI apps (Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Cursor, ChatGPT's Mac app) to read Tara's memory, they get a read-only server on your Mac that leaves out every source you switched off. What such an app looks up goes to its own AI provider with your chat; that provider's terms apply. While one is set up, the app keeps today's calendar (titles, times, places, attendees, join links) in its folder for that server.

Connectors you add yourself decide what they talk to; they are outside the scope of this statement.

International transfers. Anthropic and the other providers named above may process data in the USA.

6. Email

If you write to the address above, your email address and message are processed to answer you. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, the legitimate interest being to answer inquiries. The email is deleted once it is no longer needed, unless there is a legal obligation to keep it.

7. Your rights

You have the right of access (Art. 15 GDPR), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and to object to processing based on Art. 6(1)(f) (Art. 21). Where processing is based on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time with effect for the future (Art. 7(3)). You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, for example the Berlin Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information.
